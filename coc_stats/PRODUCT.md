# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Both clan leadership and regular members, roughly equally — neither is the primary audience with the other bolted on. Leaders use it to make roster and strategy calls (who to bench, who's carrying, war/CWL/raid planning). Regular members use it to check their own progress and standing against the rest of the clan.

## Product Purpose

Turns raw war/CWL/raid/ranked logs into automatic verdicts and judgment (who's carrying, who's coasting) instead of leaving that interpretation to the reader, and puts war, CWL, raid, ranked, and roster in one unified place instead of scattered screenshots, memory, and spreadsheets.

## Positioning

Turns raw logs into verdicts, and puts everything — war, CWL, raid, ranked, roster — in one place, instead of spreadsheets and screenshots.

## Brand Personality

Military, tactical, precise — a command-center feel. But precision must not curdle into coldness: this has to feel like a place clan members want to click through and explore, not a data dump they dread opening. The tactical framing is the flavor; inviting and explorable is the requirement it serves.

## Anti-references

The ad-hoc redesign this session already rejected (flattened everything into generic bordered stat-tile boxes — the SaaS-dashboard cliché). Also, explicitly, anything that reads as soulless or spreadsheet-like — cold rows of numbers with no personality, the "Excel sheet" feeling.

## Design Principles

Verdicts over raw numbers — every surface should tell you what a number *means*, not just report it.

One command center, not stitched-together pages — war, CWL, raid, ranked, and roster should feel like facets of one tool, not separate mini-apps that happen to share a nav bar.

Tactical, not sterile — precision and military framing are the personality, but the page should invite clicking around, never read as a cold data dump.

Both audiences, every time — a surface that only serves leadership decision-making or only serves personal progress-checking is half-built; design for both without treating either as secondary.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No unusual requirements named — hold to solid baseline practice: sufficient contrast, visible keyboard focus, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion`.
